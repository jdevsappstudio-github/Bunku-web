75% Attendance Rule Explained: UGC/AICTE Rules, Condonation & What Happens If You Fall Short

8 August 2026 · 5 min read

The "75% rule" gets repeated so often that most students have never actually seen where it comes from or what it precisely covers. Here's the real version — including the part that lets some students recover from a shortfall.

Where the rule comes from

The 75% minimum traces back to University Grants Commission (UGC) and All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) guidelines, which most Indian universities and affiliated colleges follow (some set their own, slightly different minimum — see our calculator for 65/80/85% variants). The core idea: below a certain attendance threshold, a college can't reasonably certify that you've completed the course.

It's subject-wise, not one overall number

This is the part most students get wrong. Attendance isn't a single combined percentage across your whole timetable — it's tracked per subject, and in most science/engineering programs, theory and practical attendance are tracked separately within each subject. You need 75% in both, independently. Being at 90% overall doesn't protect you if one specific subject's theory attendance has quietly slipped below 75%.

It resets every semester

Attendance percentage is calculated per semester, not cumulatively across your degree. A rough semester doesn't follow you into the next one — but it also means you can't "bank" a great semester's surplus to cover a bad one later.

What actually happens if you fall below 75%

Consequences vary by college, but the common pattern is:

  • Debarment from the semester-end exam in that specific subject — the most serious and most common outcome.
  • A short-attendance notice partway through the semester, giving you a chance to recover before it's final.
  • A fine or a formal condonation application requirement in some colleges, as a condition of still being allowed to sit the exam.

The condonation provision

Here's the part that doesn't get talked about enough: most universities allow a condonation of up to 10% — effectively dropping the real floor to 65% — for documented, valid reasons like medical leave or a family emergency. This isn't automatic. You typically need to:

  1. Submit a formal application to your department, usually before results/exam eligibility is finalized
  2. Attach real supporting documentation (medical certificate, etc.)
  3. Get it approved — colleges can and do reject weak applications

If you're sitting between 65% and 75% for a genuine reason, it's worth asking your department directly rather than assuming you're automatically out.
