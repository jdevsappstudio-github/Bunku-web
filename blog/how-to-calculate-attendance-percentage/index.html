How to Calculate Attendance Percentage in College (Formula & Examples)

8 August 2026 · 4 min read

If you've ever done the math on a scrap of paper before a class you're thinking of skipping, you already know the stakes — most Indian colleges won't let you sit the exam if your attendance drops too low. Here's the exact formula, with worked examples, so you're not guessing.

The formula

Attendance % = (Classes Attended ÷ Total Classes Held) × 100

That's it — two numbers. "Classes Held" means classes that have actually happened so far this semester, not the total scheduled for the whole term. Your percentage moves every single time a class happens, whether you're there or not.

Worked examples

Example 1 — early semester. 8 classes have been held, you attended 6. That's (6 ÷ 8) × 100 = 75% — right on the line. One more miss without an attend to balance it, and you're below.

Example 2 — mid-semester, been slacking. 40 classes held, you attended 27. That's (27 ÷ 40) × 100 = 67.5% — below the usual 75% minimum, and you'd need several attends in a row to climb back (see our bunk-margin guide for exactly how many).

Example 3 — comfortably ahead. 50 classes held, you attended 46. That's (46 ÷ 50) × 100 = 92% — well clear, with real room to skip a few before it matters.

Two mistakes that throw the number off

1. Counting scheduled classes instead of held classes

Your total isn't "how many classes are on the timetable this semester" — it's how many have actually happened so far. A cancelled class, a holiday, or a professor being out doesn't count toward the total at all.

2. Averaging theory and practical together

Most engineering and science programs track theory and lab/practical attendance separately, each needing to independently clear the minimum. Averaging them into one number can make a real shortfall in one look fine on paper — and your college's actual records won't average them for you.
